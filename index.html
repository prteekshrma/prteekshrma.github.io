About

Prateek Sharma is an Assistant Professor of Physics in Uttarakhand, India. He received his Ph.D. in Physics from Gurukula Kangri (Deemed to be University), Haridwar, in 2021, where he was a Junior Research Fellow in a SERB project on null geodesics around strong gravitational sources. His research is in general relativity and black hole phenomenology, with a focus on how black holes in modified theories of gravity can be tested through their shadows, gravitational lensing, particle orbits and thermodynamics. He teaches physics at the undergraduate and postgraduate levels and supervises M.Sc. and B.Sc. students.

Research

  • General relativity
  • Black hole shadows
  • Gravitational lensing
  • Geodesics
  • Black hole thermodynamics
  • Modified gravity

Black holes predicted by theories beyond general relativity, such as supergravity, f(R) gravity, Einstein–Yang–Mills theory and string-inspired models, or surrounded by matter fields such as quintessence, can differ measurably from the Kerr black hole of Einstein's theory. His work studies these differences through observable quantities: the size and shape of the black hole shadow, the deflection of light and weak lensing (including in a plasma), the stability of particle and photon orbits, and the thermodynamic properties of the spacetime. The aim is to connect such models with observations like those of the Event Horizon Telescope.
